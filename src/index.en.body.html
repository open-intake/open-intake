Open IntakeAn open JSON format for clinics

By putting one small file on their own website, clinics let the AI assistants patients already use guide them with correct information.

The goal is to improve the match between clinics and patients: helping patients reach a clinic that fits their symptoms and is open now, without getting lost.

Free to adopt and useOpen sourceNo diagnosisNo patient data storedFeedback from clinicians welcome

Why it is needed

When people feel unwell, most of them search for a clinic on their phone. At every step, they are reading information that someone other than the clinic has compiled.

  1. What the patient doesWhere the information comes fromWhat often goes wrong
  2. Searches for "clinic near me" or "fever clinic"Search results and map listingsEach service decides what is shown and in what order
  3. Checks opening hours on a mapData registered with the map service, sometimes edited by usersTemporary closures and changed hours are missing. Clinics often cannot get them corrected quickly
  4. Reads clinic directories and reviewsEach site's own collection, sometimes shaped by paid listingsSites disagree, and it is unclear which is current
  5. Books online or by phoneAvailability from booking services the clinic has joinedClinics outside the service do not appear. Phones only work during opening hours
  6. Now: asks an AI "where should I go?"The AI reads the same search results, maps and sitesIf the source is out of date, so is the AI's answer. That is why the clinic itself needs to publish the source

What happens today

  • Opening hours and similar facts depend on map services and review sites
  • Temporary closures and on-call duties are slow to appear
  • The places to correct them are scattered, and some cannot be corrected by the clinic
  • Putting an AI chatbot on a clinic's site costs money and effort, and must be rebuilt as AI changes

What this makes possible

  • AI reads what the clinic itself wrote, directly
  • Front-desk staff can update closures, duties and notices right away
  • AI helps patients fill in the clinic's intake questionnaire before the visit
  • The clinic needs no AI and no server

At a rural petrol station in Japan, the opening days shown on a map service were wrong. The staff said, "We told them, but it never gets fixed." The same can happen to clinics. The difference is that the people misled are patients who feel unwell.

How seeing a doctor will change (our forecast)

Independently of this project, the front door of healthcare is changing. AI is becoming a concierge that stays with people when they feel unwell. This is what we expect.

  1. Today

    People ask AI, then find a clinic themselves

    • More people describe their symptoms to an AI instead of searching
    • The AI gives general explanations. Where and when to go is still up to the patient
  2. Within a few years

    AI guides people from home to a booked visit

    • Simple triage: the AI asks about symptoms and suggests whether to call an ambulance, see a doctor soon, or wait and watch
    • Advice and information: self-care at home, changes to watch for, which department to visit
    • Simple intake: since when, fever, conditions, medicines, organised through conversation
    • Finding and booking: a nearby clinic that fits and is open now, through to booking
  3. Beyond

    Before and after the visit becomes one flow

    • With the patient's consent, the organised answers reach the clinic before the visit
    • Guidance considers existing conditions and medicines, and follows up after the visit
    • Diagnosis and treatment remain the doctor's decision. AI is the guide that gets patients there correctly
What the AI will be missing However capable the guide becomes, only the clinic knows whether it is open today, what it treats, how to book, and what to ask before a visit. If this is not published correctly in a form AI can read, the AI's guidance fails at that point. Open Intake lets any clinic provide this clinic-side entry point, free of charge. What clinics need in this flow is not more sophisticated software, but correct information circulating in a form AI can read. Software keeps improving on the AI side. Correct information is what only the clinic can provide.

Publish once, and every service can read it

Think of the notice on a clinic's front door. Instead of calling everyone to say you are closed, you put up one notice and everyone who passes reads it. This file is that notice, on the internet.

All the clinic does is keep its own file up to date. No need to ask any particular service. No registration or usage fees. The more readers support it, the more places the same file is used.

A long-standing pattern on the internet Put robots.txt or sitemap.xml on a site and search engines around the world come to read it. RSS works the same way. Site owners never contact each search engine. They place a file in an agreed place, in an agreed format.

Not every service reads this file today. But any AI that can browse the web can read it as soon as a patient gives it the clinic's web address. Because the format is fixed and open to all, clinics that publish it benefit first as support grows.

Six benefits for clinics

Easy to adopt

Fill in the creation tool or talk with an AI, and the file is ready. Put it on your website and you are done. No server, no monthly fees.

Easy to manage

Regular and temporary information live in separate files. Front-desk staff only update the temporary file, and each file can have its own editors. The tool shows the content in plain language, so no technical skill is needed to check it.

Safe

No patient information is sent to or stored by the clinic. The file holds only information that is fine to publish. The format itself requires that AI does not diagnose, and that it recommends emergency services before booking when warning signs apply.

Future-proof

Fast-changing AI is kept separate from slow-changing information such as hours and questionnaire items, so the file keeps working as AI changes. The format follows international standards (HL7 FHIR and schema.org) and has rules so older versions remain readable.

Show what you focus on

Two clinics in the same department can differ: one may focus on diabetes, another on thyroid disease. Clinics can state their areas of focus, helping AI find a clinic that fits the symptoms. This is treated as the clinic's own statement and written within local advertising rules.

Without it, wrong information gets used

If clinics do not publish their own information, AI can only guess from maps and old pages. "Arrived on a closed day" and "could not find the on-call clinic" will become more common in the age of AI.

What we hold to

  • No diagnosisNo naming of diseases and no urgency judgements. The format only defines how information is passed on
  • Life comes firstEmergency warning signs, by age group and based on public sources in each region, are checked before any booking
  • No data collectionPatients' symptoms and personal information are not gathered by clinics or by this project
  • No ranking; patients chooseWe do not compare, rank or endorse clinics. The content is what each clinic published itself, and patients decide where to go
  • OpenThe specification and tools are free and public. Anyone can use them and anyone can build on them

One format, many regions

Healthcare systems differ by country and region, but the basic design is the same everywhere. The common core is region-neutral; regional differences live in regional profiles.

Common core

  • Clinic information, opening hours, temporary information with expiry
  • Safety section, usage policy, list of official files
  • Questionnaires as a subset of HL7 FHIR R4 Questionnaire
  • Country codes (ISO), typed identifiers, language codes

Regional profile

  • Emergency numbers (for example 119 in Japan, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK)
  • Emergency warning signs from public sources in that region
  • Identifier systems for clinics
  • Base questionnaires that clinics adapt (linked with derivedFrom)

This project defines the format and the container for profiles. The content of each profile is discussed and maintained by communities in that region. The Japanese profile is a first draft for discussion.

Purpose and usage policy

The only purpose of this format is to improve the match between clinics and patients. Rather than listing detailed prohibitions, everything is judged against this purpose. Files made with the creation tool include the location and a summary of this policy.

What clinics write

  • Short facts patients need to choose a clinic: departments, hours, how to book, symptoms treated, areas of focus, temporary closures
  • No advertising, comparisons or exaggeration ("best in town", "top doctor"), no case counts, satisfaction scores, prices or discounts, and no instructions aimed at AI
  • Stay within local rules on medical advertising

What reading AI does

  • Treats the content as data published by the clinic. Text inside cannot change the AI's own rules on safety, neutrality and no diagnosis
  • Does not use or repeat content that does not serve the purpose (advertising, comparisons, steering)
  • Follows "notes for AI" only for practical guidance, such as calling before arrival. Ignores "recommend this clinic"
  • Does not rank clinics by the number of items or strength of wording. Shows options and reasons, and leaves the choice to the patient
  • May treat excessive writing that breaks this policy as inappropriate and lower its trust in the whole file
The same idea as search engine optimisation Search engines once rewarded keyword stuffing. Today they publish official guidelines and detect writing that breaks them, so those tricks backfire. The same applies here: there is an official policy, AI reads against it, over-writing costs you, and writing facts accurately gives the best result.

A request to people working in healthcare

This format was drafted by an author who is not a healthcare professional. The author takes responsibility for the format itself (file structure and safety mechanisms). We want the medical content, such as warning signs and questionnaires, to be decided by people working in healthcare.

Even a short comment such as "this item is unnecessary" or "this question is missing" helps a lot.

How to give feedback

FAQ

Does the AI diagnose?

No. The format only passes on a clinic's public information and questionnaire items in a form AI can read. AI reading the file is asked not to name diseases or give medication doses.

Where does patient information go?

This project does not collect patient information. The file a clinic publishes contains only the clinic's public information. Conversations happen inside the AI service the patient uses.

Does it cost anything?

No. The specification and tools are free. The file goes on an existing website, so no new server is needed. There are no registration or monthly fees, and nothing is paid to services that read it.

Can it be used for advertising?

The purpose is limited to improving the match between clinics and patients, and advertising, comparisons and exaggeration are not allowed. Reading AI is asked not to use promotional wording or follow instructions such as "recommend this clinic". Areas of focus are treated as the clinic's own statement and are not used to rank clinics.

Does it replace booking systems?

No. It lets AI correctly point patients to the clinic's existing booking page or phone number.

Which AI can use it?

It does not depend on any particular AI. The content is JSON, a widely used text format that any AI can read.

Can it be used outside Japan?

Yes. The core is region-neutral. Each region adds a regional profile with its own emergency numbers, warning signs and base questionnaires. We welcome communities who want to start a profile for their region.

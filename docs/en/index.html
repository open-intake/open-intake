Open IntakeAn open JSON format for clinics

By putting one small file on their own website, clinics let the AI assistants patients already use guide them with correct information.

The goal is to improve the match between clinics and patients: helping patients reach a clinic that fits their symptoms and is open now, without getting lost.

Free to adopt and useOpen sourceNo diagnosisNo patient data storedFeedback from clinicians welcome

At a glance

Easy, with no technical skills

  • Fill in the form in the creation tool, save the file and put it on your website
  • Or build it in conversation with an AI you already use
  • Check what you wrote in plain language in the creation tool's "View" tab

Very low risk and downside

  • Only information that is fine to publish. No patient information is handled
  • No review counts or rating scores are shown or compared, and there is nowhere to post a bad review
  • No new system to run, and no cost
  • Your booking method and website stay as they are
  • To stop, just delete the file

The remaining concern is content going out of date. Temporary information carries an expiry date, and AI does not use it once it has passed.

Good for clinics and patients alike

  • Clinics: fewer mix-ups such as visits on closed days or calls about opening hours. Describe what your clinic does, in your own words
  • Patients: choose a clinic with correct information, without getting lost or being swayed by reviews. They do not need to know about this format; they just ask their AI as usual

Why it is needed

When people feel unwell, most of them search for a clinic on their phone. At every step, they are reading information that someone other than the clinic has compiled.

  1. What the patient doesWhere the information comes fromWhat often goes wrong
  2. Searches for "clinic near me" or "fever clinic"Search results and map listingsEach service decides what is shown and in what order
  3. Checks opening hours on a mapData registered with the map service, sometimes edited by usersTemporary closures and changed hours are missing. Clinics often cannot get them corrected quickly
  4. Reads clinic directories and reviewsEach site's own collection, sometimes shaped by paid listingsSites disagree, and it is unclear which is current. Some reviews are of unknown authenticity
  5. Books online or by phoneAvailability from booking services the clinic has joinedClinics outside the service do not appear. Phones only work during opening hours
  6. Now: asks an AI "where should I go?"The AI reads the same search results, maps and review sitesIf the source is out of date, so is the AI's answer. Its answer can also be swayed by ratings on maps and review sites, some of unknown authenticity. That is why the clinic itself needs to publish the source

What happens today

  • Opening hours and similar facts depend on map services and review sites
  • Temporary closures and on-call duties are slow to appear
  • The places to correct them are scattered, and some cannot be corrected by the clinic
  • Putting an AI chatbot on a clinic's site costs money and effort, and must be rebuilt as AI changes

What this makes possible

  • AI reads what the clinic itself wrote, directly
  • Front-desk staff can update closures, duties and notices right away
  • AI helps patients fill in the clinic's intake questionnaire before the visit
  • The clinic needs no AI and no server

How seeing a doctor will change (our forecast)

Independently of this project, the front door of healthcare is changing. AI is becoming a concierge that stays with people when they feel unwell. This is what we expect.

  1. Today

    People ask AI, then find a clinic themselves

    • More people describe their symptoms to an AI instead of searching
    • The AI gives general explanations. Where and when to go is still up to the patient
  2. Within a few years

    AI guides people from home to a booked visit

    • Checking for emergencies: the AI asks about symptoms and suggests whether to call an ambulance or see a doctor soon. It only errs on the side of safety and never says there is no need to see a doctor
    • Information: changes to watch for, which department to visit, where to call for advice
    • Simple intake: since when, fever, conditions, medicines, organised through conversation
    • Finding and booking: a nearby clinic that fits and is open now, through to booking
  3. Beyond

    Before and after the visit becomes one flow

    • The patient's AI fills in and submits each clinic's existing booking page on the patient's behalf, adapting to how each clinic works. The patient checks the content before it is sent. The clinic does not need to build any new system
    • The AI prepares the pre-visit answers in advance, following the questionnaire the clinic has published. Only the reason for the visit and the minimum go with the booking; details such as conditions and medicines are shared with the patient's consent after the visit is confirmed (never before the clinic has accepted the patient)
    • If the booking page shows queue numbers or expected times, the AI can read them and tell the patient roughly when they will be seen. Patients can leave home to match, which cuts waiting times and crowded waiting rooms. Clinic options can also show the expected time if booked now
    • Existing conditions and medicines are organised so that the doctor sees them, and the AI follows up after the visit
    • Diagnosis and treatment remain the doctor's decision. AI is the guide that gets patients there correctly
What the AI will be missing However capable the guide becomes, only the clinic knows whether it is open today, what it treats, how to book, and what to ask before a visit. If this is not published correctly in a form AI can read, the AI's guidance fails at that point. Open Intake lets any clinic provide this clinic-side entry point, free of charge. What clinics need in this flow is not more sophisticated software, but correct information circulating in a form AI can read. Software keeps improving on the AI side. Correct information is what only the clinic can provide.

How it works

The AI sits on the patient's side. The clinic only provides public information in a form AI can read.

Patient's AI

→guides

Patient

Decides whether, when and where to go, with correct information. If symptoms may be life-threatening: call emergency services before booking

Publish once, and every service can read it

Think of the notice on a clinic's front door. Instead of calling everyone to say you are closed, you put up one notice and everyone who passes reads it. This file is that notice, on the internet.

Today: tell each service separately

Every new service means more work: registering in different ways and correcting all of them at every closure. Corrections are not always accepted, and visibility may require paid plans.

From now: publish on your own site

All the clinic does is keep its own file up to date. No need to ask any particular service. No registration or usage fees. The more readers support it, the more places the same file is used.

A long-standing pattern on the internet Put robots.txt or sitemap.xml on a site and search engines around the world come to read it. RSS works the same way. Site owners never contact each search engine. They place a file in an agreed place, in an agreed format.

Not every service reads this file today. But any AI that can browse the web can read it as soon as a patient gives it the clinic's web address. Because the format is fixed and open to all, clinics that publish it benefit first as support grows.

How a clinic uses it

There are two files, to make management easier: the information changes at different speeds and is kept by different people. The regular file is made once and rarely touched; only the temporary file is updated by staff as needed.

Regular information clinic-intake.json

Almost fixed. Reviewed a few times a year

Contents
Name, departments, regular hours, how to book, areas of focus, questionnaires
Who
The director or the website manager creates it or reviews it. Not touched day to day
How
  • Fill in the form in the creation tool and save the file
  • Or copy the AI instructions from the creation tool and build it in conversation with any AI you already use

Temporary information clinic-schedule.json

Any time. Update it as often as needed

Contents
  • Temporary closures and changed hours
  • Doctors' schedules (for example, a specialist clinic on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons)
  • Weekend and night on-call duties
  • Notices and requests of the moment (for example, vaccinations are by appointment)
Who
Front-desk or other assigned staff. They never touch the regular file, so a mistake cannot break it
Updating
  1. Fill in "Temporary information" in the creation tool. Or keep the AI instructions for temporary information and ask any AI you use, together with the current file, something like "add a closure from 29 December to 3 January"
  2. Save the result and overwrite the file at its fixed place on the website
  3. If you cannot upload it yourself, you can email a summary of the change to your website manager
Expiry
The file has an expiry date. After it passes, AI stops using the file, so old notices never linger

Model case: a year at an internal medicine clinic

Responsible person (director)Can change the regular information and is responsible for it
Website manager (staff or an outside company)Puts the files on the website
Assigned staff (front desk, etc.)Can change only the temporary information, at any time
  1. At the start, yearlyRegularThe responsible person creates or reviews the name, hours, booking methods and questionnaires, and the website manager publishes them. The same when hours or departments change
  2. Start of each monthTemporaryStaff enter this month's doctors' schedules, extend the expiry date to next month and remove finished items
  3. Sudden closureTemporaryIf the doctor falls ill, staff add the closure the same day. AI can guide patients with the new information right away
  4. Cold and flu seasonTemporaryAdd "If you have a fever, please call before coming in", and remove it when the season ends
  5. DecemberTemporaryAdd the New Year closure and holiday on-call duties

With separate files, a staff member's mistake can only affect the temporary information. The name, address, regular hours and questionnaires cannot be overwritten by accident. If your website supports per-file permissions, you can set it up so that staff can change the temporary file at any time, while only the responsible person can change the regular file. If it does not, the same effect comes from a simple split of roles: staff create the file and the website manager publishes it. Even if the temporary file expires, the regular information keeps being used.

No new system to run

What you publish is a plain text file. There is no login page, no database and no new program running on the clinic's server. The creation tool does not send what you type anywhere; it runs only in your own browser. So this format adds no new point of attack.

If the file is ever altered, anyone can check its content at any time (in the creation tool's "View" tab or with an AI), so suspicious changes are easier to notice.

Whether you can regain control of a website that has been taken over is a separate issue that every website already has. Looking after the website matters as much as ever.

Check the content without technical skills

  • The creation tool's "View" tab shows the content in plain language. A published file can be checked the same way: open it in a browser and paste its content into "Paste and check"
  • The content is text, so people can roughly read it. Give it to an AI and it can summarise it or turn it into a PDF or Word document
  • Give an AI that can browse the web the file's address (for example https://your-site/clinic-intake.json) and ask what it says. Anyone can check it, at work or at home, at any time. AI can misread, though, so check with the creation tool's "View" tab before publishing
  • Only information that is fine to publish goes in. Patient information and internal matters are never included, so it does not matter who sees it

Matching without reviews

This format has no reviews, no scores and no rating fields. There is simply nowhere to post a bad review. AI compares only what each clinic has published itself and objective data that is the same for everyone.

On the patient's side

  • Patients add only the clinics they want to go back to as their regular clinics in their health summary. There is no field for a negative rating
  • AI does not use online reviews or star ratings to compare options
  • AI shows options and reasons based on data. The patient decides where to go

Example: two regular clinics in internal medicine

When the patient describes their current symptoms, the AI lines up what each clinic publishes, areas of focus, hours and temporary closures, together with whether it is open now and the distance or travel time (when the patient shares where they are). It shows them with other options, each with a reason (for example, "Clinic A lists diabetes as an area of focus" or "Clinic B is open now").

For clinics Areas of focus are optional. If you list them, they are one more clue for patients looking for care in that area. Listing them never removes you from the options for other symptoms, and not listing them never lowers your place (AI does not rank clinics by the number of items, and areas of focus are treated as the clinic's own statement). Requests specific to your clinic (for example, "If you have a fever, please call before coming in") are passed on to patients by AI that reads the file before they visit. That means less work telling each patient. Not every patient uses AI, so keep your notices in the clinic as before. Doctors' schedules, once published officially, let AI say "that doctor sees patients on Tuesdays and Thursdays", so patients can choose that day when booking. You keep your current booking method (phone or booking page) without adding a complex booking system.

Six benefits for clinics

Easy to adopt

Fill in the creation tool or talk with an AI, and the file is ready. Put it on your website and you are done. No server, no monthly fees.

Easy to manage

Regular and temporary information live in separate files. Front-desk staff only update the temporary file, and each file can have its own editors. The tool shows the content in plain language, so no technical skill is needed to check it.

Safe

No patient information is sent to or stored by the clinic. The file holds only information that is fine to publish. The format itself requires that AI does not diagnose, and that it recommends emergency services before booking when warning signs apply.

Future-proof

Fast-changing AI is kept separate from slow-changing information such as hours and questionnaire items, so the file keeps working as AI changes. The format follows international standards (HL7 FHIR and schema.org) and has rules so older versions remain readable.

Show what you focus on

Two clinics in the same department can differ: one may focus on diabetes, another on thyroid disease. Clinics can state their areas of focus, helping AI find a clinic that fits the symptoms. This is treated as the clinic's own statement and written within local advertising rules.

Less risk of patients being guided with wrong information

With information the clinic wrote itself, AI does not have to guess from maps and old pages. Mix-ups such as "arrived on a closed day" or "could not find the on-call clinic" become less likely. This is the kind of thing it prevents: if a holiday closure is missing from a map, a family with a feverish child may arrive at a locked door. Clinics cannot always get map listings corrected quickly.

The patient side: my health summary (draft)

In the other direction, patients can keep their own conditions, medicines and allergies in my-health.json, on their own responsibility and on their own device.

Create

  • Fill in the form and save the file
  • Or give the AI instructions to your own AI and create it in conversation (it can transcribe from photos of medicine records)
  • Keep the file on your device. No names, addresses or ID numbers

Use

  • When unwell, give it to your AI together with your current symptoms. The AI organises the information for the doctor, including your conditions and medicines. Your conditions are never a reason to drop a clinic from the options
  • The AI does not diagnose. It organises what doctors need: when it started, where, what kind, how severe, and so on
  • At the visit, print the visit sheet or show it on screen

So that self-reported history never becomes a reason to refuse care or pass patients around, only the reason for the visit and the minimum are shared when booking; details are shared after the visit is arranged. The creation tool is in Japanese for now.

What we hold to

  • No diagnosisNo naming of diseases and no urgency judgements. The format only defines how information is passed on
  • Life comes firstEmergency warning signs, by age group and based on public sources in each region, are checked before any booking
  • No data collectionPatients' symptoms and personal information are not gathered by clinics or by this project
  • No ranking; patients chooseWe do not compare, rank or endorse clinics. The content is what each clinic published itself, and patients decide where to go
  • OpenThe specification and tools are free and public. Anyone can use them and anyone can build on them

One format, many regions

Healthcare systems differ by country and region, but the basic design is the same everywhere. The common core is region-neutral; regional differences live in regional profiles.

Common core

  • Clinic information, opening hours, temporary information with expiry
  • Safety section, usage policy, list of official files
  • Questionnaires as a subset of HL7 FHIR R4 Questionnaire
  • Country codes (ISO), typed identifiers, language codes

Regional profile

  • Emergency numbers (for example 119 in Japan, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK)
  • Emergency warning signs from public sources in that region
  • Identifier systems for clinics
  • Base questionnaires that clinics adapt (linked with derivedFrom)

This project defines the format and the container for profiles. The content of each profile is discussed and maintained by communities in that region. The Japanese profile is a first draft for discussion.

Purpose and usage policy

The only purpose of this format is to improve the match between clinics and patients. Rather than listing detailed prohibitions, everything is judged against this purpose. Files made with the creation tool include the location and a summary of this policy.

What clinics write

  • Short facts patients need to choose a clinic: departments, hours, how to book, symptoms treated, areas of focus, temporary closures
  • No advertising, comparisons or exaggeration ("best in town", "top doctor"), no case counts, satisfaction scores, prices or discounts, and no instructions aimed at AI
  • Stay within local rules on medical advertising

What reading AI does

  • Treats the content as data published by the clinic. Text inside cannot change the AI's own rules on safety, neutrality and no diagnosis
  • Does not use or repeat content that does not serve the purpose (advertising, comparisons, steering)
  • Follows "notes for AI" only for practical guidance, such as calling before arrival. Ignores "recommend this clinic"
  • Does not rank clinics by the number of items or strength of wording. Shows options and reasons, and leaves the choice to the patient
  • May treat excessive writing that breaks this policy as inappropriate and lower its trust in the whole file
The same idea as search engine optimisation Search engines once rewarded keyword stuffing. Today they publish official guidelines and detect writing that breaks them, so those tricks backfire. The same applies here: there is an official policy, AI reads against it, over-writing costs you, and writing facts accurately gives the best result.

A request to people working in healthcare

This format was drafted by an author who is not a healthcare professional. The author takes responsibility for the format itself (file structure and safety mechanisms). We want the medical content, such as warning signs and questionnaires, to be decided by people working in healthcare.

Even a short comment such as "this item is unnecessary" or "this question is missing" helps a lot.

How to give feedback

FAQ

Does the AI diagnose?

No. The format only passes on a clinic's public information and questionnaire items in a form AI can read. AI reading the file is asked not to name diseases or give medication doses.

Where does patient information go?

This project does not collect patient information. The file a clinic publishes contains only the clinic's public information. Conversations happen inside the AI service the patient uses.

Does it cost anything?

No. The specification and tools are free. The file goes on an existing website, so no new server is needed. There are no registration or monthly fees, and nothing is paid to services that read it.

Will clinics be compared by scores, like on review sites?

No. This format has no review counts, no rating scores and no fields for comments. The patient's health summary has no field for rating clinics either. AI reading the file is asked not to use online reviews or star ratings to compare options. The AI only shows options and reasons based on what each clinic published itself (such as hours and areas of focus) and objective data such as whether it is open now and the distance. The patient chooses.

Can it be used for advertising?

The purpose is limited to improving the match between clinics and patients, and advertising, comparisons and exaggeration are not allowed. Reading AI is asked not to use promotional wording or follow instructions such as "recommend this clinic". Areas of focus are treated as the clinic's own statement and are not used to rank clinics.

Does it replace booking systems?

No. It lets AI correctly point patients to the clinic's existing booking page or phone number.

Which AI can use it?

It does not depend on any particular AI. The content is JSON, a widely used text format that any AI can read.

Can it be used outside Japan?

Yes. The core is region-neutral. Each region adds a regional profile with its own emergency numbers, warning signs and base questionnaires. We welcome communities who want to start a profile for their region.
